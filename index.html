About

A bit about me

I'm a PhD student in the Department of Civil & Environmental Engineering at Louisiana State University, where I've been a graduate research assistant since August 2023, working with Dr. William M. Moe on biological waste treatment.

Wastewater treatment leans on communities of bacteria to do the actual chemistry — pulling nitrogen out of the water, storing carbon, converting what would otherwise be a pollutant. But a good number of the organisms involved are still poorly characterised, which makes it hard to say what they are capable of. My days are largely spent at the bench: growing and subculturing strains, staining and looking at them down a microscope, extracting DNA, running PCR and gels — the patient work of establishing what an organism actually is before anyone can say what it does.

I came to this from chemical engineering. My BSc is from the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology, where I worked on cleaner production in the power sector and spent two years in a biofuel project. That grounding in process thinking is still how I approach treatment systems — as processes to be understood, tuned, and scaled.

Research & Publications

I work on biological waste treatment at Louisiana State University, in Dr. William M. Moe's group. Treatment systems depend on bacteria to do the real chemistry, yet many of the organisms involved remain poorly characterised. My work is about closing that gap.

01

Cultivating the organisms

Isolating and maintaining bacterial strains through cultures and subcultures — the slow, unglamorous groundwork everything else depends on.

02

Characterising them

Gram staining, microscopy and taxonomic wet-lab assays, alongside DNA extraction, PCR and gel electrophoresis, to work out what a strain actually is.

03

Publishing the reference

That characterisation feeds complete genome announcements, giving the field better reference data for organisms found in treatment systems.
