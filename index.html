About

I'm a PhD student in the Department of Civil & Environmental Engineering at Louisiana State University and a graduate research assistant in Dr. William M. Moe's research group, working on biological waste treatment.

My work combines cultivation-based microbiology, microbial enumeration, physiological characterization, molecular methods and genomic analysis. I use these to study microbial populations and to characterize bacterial isolates relevant to environmental engineering.

I earned my BSc in Chemical Engineering at the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET), where my earlier research covered cleaner production in the power sector and a microalgal biofuel project. That background gives me a process-engineering view of biological treatment.

Research

My doctoral research covers three connected areas within biological waste treatment.

Biological waste treatment and environmental microbiology

Microbial populations and processes in wastewater treatment and nutrient transformation, studied with cultivation-based and molecular approaches.

Contaminant biodegradation and bioremediation

Microbial populations associated with contaminant biodegradation, assessed through cultivation-based experiments, and characterization of the bacterial isolates involved.

Microbial genomics and bacterial characterization

Phenotypic characterization, 16S rRNA gene analysis, whole-genome analysis and comparative genomics to characterize bacterial isolates, work out taxonomic relationships and interpret genotype–phenotype patterns.
