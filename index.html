About

A bit about me

I'm a PhD student in the Department of Civil & Environmental Engineering at Louisiana State University, where I've been a graduate research assistant since August 2023, working with Dr. William M. Moe on environmental microbiology and biological treatment.

My research is on environmental bacteria — isolating and cultivating strains, characterizing them phenotypically, and combining 16S rRNA sequencing with whole-genome and comparative genomic analysis to place them taxonomically. The aim is to connect genotype, phenotype, and environmental function: to establish what an organism is, and what it is able to do in the environments where it occurs. Designing the experiments that answer those questions matters more than any single method, though the bench work behind them — cultivation, microscopy, staining, DNA extraction, PCR, and sequence analysis — is where most of the evidence comes from.

I came to this from chemical engineering. My BSc is from the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology, where I worked on cleaner production in the power sector and spent two years in a biofuel project. That grounding in process thinking still shapes how I approach biological treatment — as processes to be understood, tuned, and scaled.

Research

Research & Publications

I work in Dr. William M. Moe's group at Louisiana State University on environmental microbiology and biological treatment. Many environmentally relevant bacteria remain poorly characterized, which limits what can be said about their physiology and function. My work spans three connected themes.

01

Environmental bacterial discovery and characterization

Isolation and polyphasic characterization of environmental bacterial strains using cultivation, physiological and biochemical testing, microscopy, and 16S rRNA sequencing.

02

Microbial genomics and bacterial taxonomy

Genome assembly and analysis, comparative genomics, phylogenetic analysis, and ANI-based comparisons, interpreted alongside phenotype for bacterial classification.

03

Biological treatment and biodegradation

Environmental microorganisms relevant to nutrient transformation, contaminant biodegradation, biological treatment processes, and environmental biotechnology.
